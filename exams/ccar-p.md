# CCAR-P: Claude Certified Architect – Professional

[CCAR-F](ccar-f.md) · [CCDV-F](ccdv-f.md) · [CCAO-F](ccao-f.md) · [CCAR-P](ccar-p.md) · [Handbook home](../README.md)

CCAR-P (Claude Certified Architect – Professional) is the advanced tier of Anthropic's architect certification track. The exam is 63 questions in 120 minutes, proctored, delivered by Pearson VUE, with a passing score of 720 on a 100–1,000 scale and a reported $175 fee per attempt.

Against the rest of the program: CCAR-P sits on top of CCAR-F, extending the same blueprint into governance, stakeholder communication, and lifecycle management. It is the only exam aimed at senior and principal architects; everything else in the program is a foundations tier.

Facts checked August 28, 2026 against the Pearson VUE program listing and the published exam guide. Longer guide with sources: [youraidept.com/network/ccar-p](https://youraidept.com/network/ccar-p).

## Who it is for

Senior and principal architects who own Claude systems end to end: the design, the governance around it, and the client or executive conversations that keep it funded. No prerequisite is enforced, but sitting CCAR-F first is the sensible order unless your hands-on experience is deep and current.

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|

The program has not published official domain weights for CCAR-P yet; this is the reported structure.

## What each domain covers

What to know before you sit, organized by the published blueprint. Nothing here describes an exam question.

### The five CCAR-F foundations domains

- Everything on the CCAR-F blueprint, extended to harder scenarios: multi-agent systems with real failure modes, tool ecosystems rather than single tools, and context strategies for long-running work. See the [CCAR-F guide](ccar-f.md).

### Governance, safety, and risk management

- Designing approval gates, audit trails, and rollback into an agent system rather than bolting them on.
- Risk assessment for autonomous actions: what the system is allowed to do without a human, and how that boundary is enforced in code and configuration.
- Data handling, retention, and policy compliance across an engagement.

### Stakeholder communication and lifecycle management

- Explaining an architecture and its risks to executives and clients in their terms: cost, reliability, what can go wrong, and what it would take to fix.
- Lifecycle: evaluation before launch, monitoring after, model upgrades, prompt regression testing, and retiring what no longer earns its cost.

## Prep notes

- Everything on the CCAR-F blueprint still applies, extended to harder scenarios. If Foundations material is shaky, start there.
- The added domains are about running systems responsibly and communicating them: governance, risk, stakeholder management, and lifecycle. Delivery experience matters more than reading for these.
- Official domain weights are not published yet. Until they are, prepare breadth-first across the reported structure rather than gaming percentages.

## Common mistakes

- Sitting CCAR-P with shaky Foundations material, since the professional tier extends the same blueprint into harder scenarios.
- Preparing only from documentation. The added domains reward delivery experience, meaning systems you have run in production and had to explain to whoever paid for them.
- Gaming domain percentages that have not been published. Prepare breadth-first across the reported structure.

## A study path

1. Confirm your CCAR-F material is solid, ideally by passing it first.
2. For one system you have delivered, write the governance design, the risk register, and the executive summary you would present. Do the same for a system you would design differently today.
3. Partner Academy prep course, then practice against scenario questions that ask for trade-offs rather than definitions.
