# CCAO-F: Claude Certified Associate – Foundations

[CCAR-F](ccar-f.md) · [CCDV-F](ccdv-f.md) · [CCAO-F](ccao-f.md) · [CCAR-P](ccar-p.md) · [Handbook home](../README.md)

CCAO-F (Claude Certified Associate – Foundations) is Anthropic's certification for professionals who use Claude in daily work without writing code. The exam is 60 questions in 120 minutes, proctored, delivered by Pearson VUE, with a passing score of 720 on a 100–1,000 scale and a reported $99 fee per attempt.

Against the rest of the program: this is the only exam that requires no engineering background. CCAR-F and CCDV-F are for engineers, and CCAR-P is the senior architect tier. For operators, marketers, PMs, and analysts who want a verifiable Claude credential, CCAO-F is the entire conversation.

Facts checked August 28, 2026 against the Pearson VUE program listing and the published exam guide. Longer guide with sources: [youraidept.com/network/ccao-f](https://youraidept.com/network/ccao-f).

## Who it is for

Day-to-day Claude users in non-engineering roles: operations, marketing, project management, education, communications, and general knowledge work. The heaviest domain is output evaluation, which says a lot about what the program thinks competent non-technical use looks like.

## What each domain covers

What to know before you sit, organized by the published blueprint. Nothing here describes an exam question.

### Output evaluation and validation

- Checking facts, numbers, and citations before you use them. Models can be wrong while sounding certain, and the biggest domain on the exam is about catching that.
- Asking for sources and quotes, then verifying them against the original material.
- Recognizing when a task needs a human review step and when the output can go straight to use.

### Workflow integration and solution design

- Where Claude fits in an existing process: drafting, summarizing, classifying, extracting, and where it should not be the last step.
- Connecting Claude to the tools you already use through integrations and connectors, and what data those connections expose.

### Governance, risk, and responsible use

- Confidential and personal data: what you can paste, what you cannot, and how organization settings and policies apply.
- Bias, fairness, and the limits of the model's knowledge; acceptable use policies; keeping a human accountable for decisions.

### Prompting and task execution

- Clear task statements, context, constraints, and examples. Iterating on a prompt instead of accepting the first answer.
- Breaking large tasks into steps and giving the model the material it needs.

### Product and model selection

- Claude.ai plans and features, the model tiers and what each is good for, and when a task calls for Claude Code or an integration instead of chat.

### Configuration and knowledge management

- Projects, project instructions, knowledge files, memory, and skills: how to set up a workspace so the model has the right standing context.

### Troubleshooting and optimization

- Why an answer went wrong: missing context, an ambiguous ask, a context limit, a refusal, or a wrong tool for the job. What to change first.

## Prep notes

- The biggest domain is evaluating and validating Claude's output. Practice catching wrong answers as much as writing prompts.
- Governance and responsible use carry 15% of the marks. If your organization has AI-use policies, the reasoning behind them is exam material.
- No coding is required anywhere on the blueprint. Do not lose time on API material that belongs to the developer exam.

## Common mistakes

- Spending time on API material when nothing on this blueprint requires code.
- Treating prompting as the whole exam, when evaluating output carries more marks (21%) than writing prompts (14%).
- Skipping governance because it reads like policy. It is 15% of the exam.

## A study path

1. Week 1: read the exam guide. For a week, verify every factual claim Claude gives you at work and note what kinds of errors you find.
2. Week 2: set up a Project with instructions and knowledge files for a real task. Compare results with and without the setup.
3. Week 3: read your organization's AI-use policy and Anthropic's usage policy. Work through the Partner Academy prep course.
4. Week 4: practice exam and closed-book review.
